# Crucified: Presentation Script (5–7 min)

Built from our group's design doc and our ideas. Four speakers, about 1.5 minutes each. Reword anything so it sounds like you. Have the game open and screen-shared the whole time, with **Presenter Mode ON** on the title screen.

---

## P1: What the game is + our message (~1:15)
*[On the title screen]*

"Our game is called **Crucified**. It's a horror detective visual novel based on *The Crucible*.

You play a priest-detective who gets a letter in the middle of the night saying a demon is hiding in a small town. You have two days to question the townspeople and find it.

The twist is that **there is no demon.** Every accusation in the town comes from fear, shame, grudges, or people protecting themselves, just like Salem.

Our audience is you guys, students who just read the play. Our purpose was to make you *experience* how accusation spreads instead of just reading about it. You're the investigator, so every accusation in the game is one *you* help make.

Our message is: **fear turns ordinary people into accusers, and a panicked community rewards certainty over truth.** Every mechanic in the game is built to push that message."

---

## P2: How the game works, as one system (~1:45)
*[Start a new game; click through to the map]*

"The game runs in one loop, and each part of it pushes the player toward accusing someone.

**1. Questioning.** During the day you pick a place on the map and question whoever's there. Every question costs **one hour**, and you only get **six hours a day**. That time limit is on purpose. Salem's trials were rushed, and pressure makes people judge fast.

**2. Three ways to ask.** For every question you choose **Kind, Direct, or Intimidate**. Each character reacts differently, kind of like a Pokémon type chart. *[Pick Intimidate on Abutit]* And watch: Intimidate says **'SUPER effective!'** You get a dramatic confession fast. But **every answer you get from intimidation is a lie.** That's how Tituba's confession worked in the play: she said what they wanted to hear because she was scared.

**3. The journal.** *[Open the journal with J]* Everything you learn gets sorted into **Fact, Testimony, or Connection.** Testimony can be false, so the player has to judge whose word to trust, which is exactly what Salem's court didn't do with spectral evidence.

**4. Town meetings.** At the end of each day the whole town meets and you choose what to tell them. Calm facts help a little. Scary claims make it way worse. Even true evidence gets twisted: when you report chicken blood in a pot, the town only hears 'BLOOD.'"

---

## P3: The hysteria meter + the night (~1:30)
*[Point to the meter in the corner]*

"This is the **hysteria meter**, and it's the heart of the game. Every conversation moves it. Gentle questions calm people down, and forced confessions and shocking evidence spread rumors. You'll see pop-ups as the town gets worse: doors getting locked, marks painted on doors, a gallows being built.

But the meter has a **floor that keeps rising**, so **it always hits 100% by the trial, no matter what you do.** That's on purpose. We wanted to show that hysteria isn't one villain's fault. It's everyone's fear added together, and one reasonable person can slow it down but can't stop it.

What you *do* still matters, though. The game tracks how much calm you spread compared to how much fear, and that changes your ending.

**The night.** Between the two days you search one location. The detective has hallucinations: he saw a 'demon' kill his parents as a kid, so he sees horns in every shadow. *[If you can, show the night shadow]* We made the art pixelated on purpose. It's unclear, so the player fills in the blanks with fear, the same way the detective does and the same way Salem did."

---

## P4: The trial + endings + wrap-up (~1:30)
*[Skip to or describe the courtroom]*

"Everything ends at the **trial**. The judge, Judge Fanforth, based on Danforth, says the real line from the play: *'A person is either with this court or he must be counted against it, there be no road between.'*

You can **accuse someone**, and an innocent person hangs, then the game shows you the truth about them. Or you can say **'there is no demon,'** and the whole town turns on *you*, and the detective gets thrown in jail. **There is no good ending.** We took away the neutral option on purpose so the player feels the same trap the characters in Salem were in.

That's why it's called *Crucified*: the person who tells the truth is the one who gets punished.

We put **six real quotes from *The Crucible*** in the game, on the title cards, in character dialogue, and in the trial.

So to sum up: the questioning rewards fear, the meter shows panic is unstoppable, and the trial punishes the truth. Together they show our message: **fear turns ordinary people into accusers.** Thanks! Any questions?"

## Likely questions + who answers
| Question | Answer (1–2 sentences) |
|---|---|
| "Why can't you win?" | Because Salem didn't have a winner. A good ending would tell players the system works if you're smart enough, and our message is that it doesn't. |
| "Why make Intimidate 'super effective'?" | 'Super effective' feels like a reward in games, so players get tempted to use fear. Then they find out everything they got was a lie. |
| "Why is the meter always 100%?" | To show hysteria is a system, not one person. You can slow it, not stop it, like Hale quitting the court and the trials going on anyway. |
| "Why pixel art?" | Pixel art leaves things unclear, so the player's brain fills in the blanks, just like how the town fills gaps with fear. |
| "Who's each character based on?" | Every name is a twisted version of a Crucible character (Wibigail = Abigail, Jroctor = John Proctor, Abutit = Tituba), and each one represents a theme: fear, shame, resentment, grudges, and so on. |
| "Did you use AI?" | (Answer honestly, in line with what Ms. Cheung approved: the ideas and design are ours, and we used Claude to help build it.) |
